How to make a Scrolli scroll

A Scrolli scroll is one long strip of paper that a child rolls past a window, one scene at a time. Design it as a vertical strip: the start is at the top. Then test it in the tester.

2 · The layout, top to bottom

  • Each scene is 121 × 150 mm. That's exactly one window view.
  • Safe zone: keep words, faces and anything important at least 8 mm inside each scene (the green box, 104 × 134 mm). The window has rounded corners.
  • Bleed: extend backgrounds 3 mm past the left and right edges.
  • Blank leads: the first and last 125 mm wrap around the dowels. Keep them empty: plain paper or a soft colour, no art.
  • Art can flow across scene lines. A continuous picture feels magical when it rolls. Just keep text inside one scene.

Put your art on the ARTWORK layer and hide GUIDES before you export.

4 · Two ways to test

  • One long image: export the whole strip as PNG or JPG at 150 dpi (750 px wide).
  • Scene by scene (easier on phones and tablets): export each scene as its own image, 121 × 150 mm (715 × 886 px at 150 dpi), named 01.png, 02.png … in reading order.

Everything you load into the tester stays on your device. Nothing is uploaded or stored, and closing the tab clears it.

Adding words in the tester

  • In the tester, switch to Add words. Pick a scene, press + Add words, type, then drag the box to place it. The orange handle sets the width.
  • Choose the size (in pt), line spacing, colour, bold and alignment. Add your own font file (.ttf, .otf, .woff) and it appears in the font list.
  • A box turns red when it leaves the safe zone.
  • Switch back to Roll it to read it through the window.
  • Save words file keeps your layout (nothing is stored online). Send that file plus your font files with your art for print.

5 · Writing for a scroll

  • One idea per scroll. Journeys, parades, chases, things that grow or change, and timelines all work well, because the reader always moves forward.
  • End each scene with a hook: something peeking in from the bottom edge so the child wants to turn the dial.
  • Words per scene: ages 3–5 up to 15 · 6–8 up to 25 · 9–12 up to 40.
  • Rhythm: set up (first scenes), build with a surprise every 3–4 scenes, the big moment near the end, then a calm close.
  • Leave one or two wordless scenes for the child to tell the story.
  • Scene 1 = title, author and illustrator. Last scene = ending, credits and one question to talk about.

6 · Text and type

  • Main reading text at least 14 pt (about 5 mm cap height); headings as big as you like.
  • Use fonts whose licence allows commercial printing (for example Google Fonts / SIL OFL). Convert text to outlines in the print file.
  • Strong contrast between text and background.

7 · Before you hand in for print

  • 300 dpi, 127 mm wide including bleed, RGB or the printer's CMYK profile.
  • Everything important inside the safe zones; blank leads clean.
  • Rolled it all the way through in the tester: no text cut by the window corners.
  • Your own original work, or licensed images and fonts.
